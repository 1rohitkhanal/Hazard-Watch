Emergency prescriptions

Know what to do
before it gets worse.

Practical, easy-to-follow actions for three major hazards: floods, earthquakes, and spreading fires. Stay calm, protect yourself, and follow instructions from emergency authorities.

IF THERE IS IMMEDIATE DANGER: Move to a safer location first and contact your local emergency services when it is safe to do so. Do not put yourself at risk to rescue property.
🌊 Flood Before · During · After ◈ Earthquake Before · During · After 🔥 Fire Spread Before · During · After
🌊

Flood

Fast-moving water can become dangerous with little warning.

✓ DO

  • Move to higher ground when flooding is expected or water begins rising.
  • Follow evacuation orders and use official routes.
  • Keep an emergency kit, drinking water, medicines, and important documents ready.
  • Switch off electricity only if you can do so safely and without entering water.
  • Stay informed through official weather and emergency alerts.

✕ DON'T

  • Never walk or drive through floodwater.
  • Do not touch electrical equipment if you are wet or standing in water.
  • Do not return home until authorities say it is safe.
  • Do not drink floodwater; it may be contaminated.
  • Do not approach damaged bridges, roads, or fast-moving water.
Priority: If floodwater is rising around you, get to higher ground immediately. Do not wait for the water to become deep before leaving.
◈

Earthquake

Protect your head and body during shaking, then move carefully.

✓ DURING — DO

  • Drop, Cover, and Hold On. Get low, take cover under a sturdy desk or table, and hold on.
  • Stay away from windows, glass, shelves, and objects that can fall.
  • If indoors, stay inside until the shaking stops unless the building becomes unsafe.
  • If outdoors, move away from buildings, trees, poles, and power lines.
  • If driving, stop in a safe place away from bridges, buildings, and power lines.

✕ DON'T

  • Do not run outside during strong shaking.
  • Do not use elevators during or immediately after an earthquake.
  • Do not stand in a doorway unless it is specifically designed as a safe structural shelter.
  • Do not touch fallen power lines or damaged electrical equipment.
  • Be prepared for aftershocks and additional hazards.

✓ AFTER — DO

  • Check yourself and others for injuries if it is safe.
  • Leave badly damaged buildings and move to an open, safer area.
  • Listen to official instructions and emergency broadcasts.
  • Use text messages when possible if phone networks are congested.

✕ AFTER — DON'T

  • Do not enter visibly damaged buildings.
  • Do not light matches or use flames if you suspect a gas leak.
  • Do not spread unverified information about missing people or damage.
Priority: During shaking, protect yourself first. The standard immediate response is Drop, Cover, and Hold On.
🔥

Fire Spread

Smoke can be dangerous quickly. Early evacuation is often critical.

✓ DO

  • Raise the alarm and call local emergency services when safe to do so.
  • Leave using the nearest safe exit and follow evacuation instructions.
  • Stay low if smoke is present and use a clean cloth only as a limited aid; it is not a substitute for proper respiratory protection.
  • Close doors behind you if you can do so safely to help slow fire and smoke spread.
  • If your clothes catch fire: stop, drop, and roll.

✕ DON'T

  • Never use an elevator during a building fire.
  • Do not go back inside for phones, documents, pets, or other belongings.
  • Do not open a door if it is hot; use another exit if available.
  • Do not hide in a room without communicating your location to rescuers if you cannot escape.
  • Do not attempt to fight a large or spreading fire yourself.

✓ IF TRAPPED

  • Stay in a room with the door closed if escaping is impossible.
  • Block gaps around the door with clothing or fabric if smoke is entering.
  • Call emergency services and clearly give your location.
  • Signal from a window if it can be done safely without exposing yourself to smoke or flames.

✕ AVOID

  • Do not break windows unless necessary for escape; broken glass can cause injury and may worsen conditions.
  • Do not crawl toward visible flames or smoke to look for another route.
  • Do not re-enter until firefighters or authorities declare the area safe.
Priority: Get out, stay out, and alert emergency responders. Property can be replaced; your safety comes first.
